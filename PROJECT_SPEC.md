# Jules Berne’s Pie Chart — Project Specification

**Status:** Agreed product requirements, with implementation status noted separately below.  
**Current prototype:** v0.1.039  
**Project/package name:** `PieChart`

## 1. Purpose and approach

A free, phone-first personal nutrition, activity, and weight tracker, designed primarily for everyday use by one person, with a second user able to test and maintain it. It replaces the practical tracking functions of Noom, not its coaching or educational content. Everyday recording should be quick; the app should not require routine database administration.

Develop the web app on GitHub Pages first, then provide Android integration where browser capabilities are insufficient. Keep the interface lean, touch-first, and consistent with the existing approved design. Do not introduce dashboards, controls, or complexity without a clear need.

## 2. Home screen and charts

**The home screen is the graph**, with a compact title bar featuring the approved right-facing meat-pie slice on a nautical chart, the name **Jules Berne’s Pie Chart**, and settings. The installed-app icon uses the same artwork. Enlarge the title, logo, and settings control to use available room **within the existing title-bar height**, without reducing graph space. The Fats metric uses a visibly fatty/oily icon, distinct from Water.

Eight independent, vertically stacked chart bands appear by default, in this order: **Weight, Steps, Water, Calories, Fibre, Protein, Fats, Carbs**. Each has its own native-unit vertical scale, a distinct existing colour, fine daily marks with short downward fades, and thin full-width separators. The integrated right-hand label column shows the metric, actual reading, applicable target/budget, and a prominent percentage when a valid target exists. The far-right, 12 px colour strip has a muted full-height background and a saturated fill proportional to target achievement. Do **not** invent fat or carbohydrate targets. Weight percentage represents actual weight divided by target weight, not weight-loss progress.

The graph plots **individual daily records at every date range**, never aggregated multi-day averages. Initially show 100 days of the available history. Horizontal swipes reveal more or less historical data; the time axis moves during gestures, while the graph redraws on release. The axis marks actual calendar months, switching to year markers for long spans. The last completed day’s date belongs directly beneath its final plotted day column. Today's incomplete values appear in the right-hand readouts, **not** as a misleading partially completed chart column. There is no period selector.

Weight is shown using **actual weigh-in dots** and a smooth filled high/low envelope derived from a seven-calendar-day window of genuine measurements, including context preceding the displayed period. Never invent missed weigh-ins. Calorie ranges and valid goals use the existing coloured dashed styling. Preserve the currently approved band geometry and graph treatment unless a change is specifically requested.

Both portrait and landscape use the **same upright, left-to-right chart**, scaled to fit. Users can hide/show individual bands with explicit eye controls and reorder them by long-pressing and dragging their right-hand labels. A plain label tap must not accidentally change visibility. The installed PWA should request fullscreen without locking orientation.

## 3. Recording and daily history

- **Weight:** Double-tap its chart label to open the existing spacious entry sheet. A single horizontal, speed-sensitive ruler starts from the previous measurement; slow swipes permit precise **0.01 kg** adjustments, and fast swipes cover more ground with momentum. The fixed full reading sits directly beneath the pointer. The screen has **Record** and **Cancel** only. Preserve irregular weigh-in history and allow corrections/deletions.
- **Water:** Double-tap to show **five most recently used amounts** and **New amount**, displayed as a balanced 3×2 grid. Selecting a remembered amount records it immediately. New quantities become recent choices.
- **Interaction:** A chart-label double-tap opens one intended entry sheet; neither tap may activate an action within the newly opened sheet.
- **Food:** Search and recording will be built separately. The final entry flow should favour relevant saved Foods, Dishes, and Meals and require minimal taps; its precise screen design is not yet agreed. Existing nutrition-metric double taps currently open the **library**, not a working food diary. Recipe creation and editing are distinct from diary recording.
- **Exercise:** Record activity type, duration/time, and intensity when relevant; calculate estimated energy use, rather than requiring manually supplied calories burned. Support favourite activities. Avoid double-counting exercise and steps.
- **Diary:** Recorded intake appears as a straightforward chronological list. No compulsory breakfast/lunch/dinner categories or meal times. Historical entries can be corrected.

Steps must be obtained automatically from a compatible Android source (planned Health Connect), with **no manual step-entry screen** and no accessory requirement.

## 4. Approved food library

The approved library contains **only food records individually reviewed and explicitly approved by the users**. It must never be prepopulated with generic sample foods or bulk imports from nutritional reference datasets. During development, user-supplied exports of *already approved* items may be bundled to initialise additional test devices; those records are not new approvals.

The Food Library screen contains **one typeahead input for food names or barcode numbers, a Scan button, and a single scrolling results list**. Do not add explanatory paragraphs, provider sections, separate result headings, cards, or additional action panels. Display matching approved foods first while relevant, followed by labelled NZ, Australian, USDA, and barcode suggestions as applicable. Show the source as a small unobtrusive label beside each external result. Show approved records as **Food Item**, **Dish**, or **Meal**, using distinct colours already present in the graph (green, orange, and purple). The list ends with **Make a Dish** and **Put together a Meal** actions; do not show a Create new Food Item button in the library. Search results and stored Food Item, Dish, and Meal names use **Capital Case**, never full capitals or arbitrary source casing. The results area keeps its size while typing. The NZ Food Composition Database is the primary reference for ordinary foods. Bundle **read-only, locally searchable reference indexes** from the NZ Concise Food Composition Tables 14th edition (2021, **1,278** distinct per-100 g records), Australian Food Composition Database Release 3 (**1,588**, including per-100 mL liquids), and USDA Foundation Foods October 2026 (**424**) in that order. All **3,290** records remain outside the approved library; users need not download or import them. Each source must remain visually identifiable and retain attribution, date, and original source references. The live NZ online database may be newer, but has no verified supported public search API.

For packaged foods, support typing or scanning barcodes and prefill name, brand, barcode, and straightforward pack-size conversions when available. Label any barcode data source (currently Open Food Facts as an expressly identified public fallback), and emphasise that **successful barcode identification does not guarantee correct nutrition**. Source values may be incomplete, outdated, or wrongly transcribed; provide editable nutrition fields and require checking and explicit approval. FoodSwitch and GS1 data are not free programmatic sources unless access is verified. No reference or barcode source can populate the approved library automatically. Australian data attribution and licence notices must be maintained; USDA Foundation data are public domain. Preserve incomplete USDA nutrients as missing, never as implicit zero.

Selecting any external suggestion opens an editable review form. **Only explicit approval saves an item** to the shared library. Product-label capture is a two-step option: scan a barcode to identify a packaged product, then use **Scan nutrition panel** on its review form to photograph or choose a label image. On-device OCR searches the photograph for **nutrient names first**; uses their positions to locate the panel and, where possible, rereads that region in table mode. It identifies the relevant **per-100 g/mL column by its heading and horizontal position**, then reads the intersections of nutrient rows with that column, regardless of nutrient order or intervening extra nutrients. If the heading is unclear but nutrient rows are recognisable, show the photograph and allow the user to tap the per-100 column. Never assume the last column or OCR text sequence is correct. It must not silently accept ambiguous values (e.g. an OCR `g` misread as a trailing `9`); unresolved values remain editable and require manual checking. The app never saves serving-column figures as per-100 values. The review form highlights values read from the photo, leaves unclear/missing fields for manual correction, and requires the user to verify the actual label before approving. The photo is not uploaded to any server or retained in the database; initial OCR model files may require an internet connection. Provide source information, allow manually entering a specific Food Item, and never silently populate the library or replace NZ name-search data with a different provider.

Every approved Food Item must have **energy, protein, carbohydrates, fats, and fibre**, normally per **100 g or 100 mL**. The review form is minimal and arranges nutrients **one per row in NZ food-label order**: energy, protein, fat, carbohydrate, and fibre. Energy has adjacent kJ and kcal inputs on the same row; each other nutrient has one numeric input with g units. Avoid explanatory per-field captions and clutter. Editing either energy field immediately updates the other using **1 kcal = 4.184 kJ**. Editing either energy input must immediately cross-calculate the other. Preserve legitimate small differences between both values printed on a manufacturer label; when external energy figures disagree materially (over 2%), use their kJ value to derive kcal unless the user explicitly corrects them. A missing calorie value is derived from kJ. Do not silently replace verified product-label figures or introduce explanatory field clutter. Missing figures require a reasonable, identified estimate rather than omission or an unlabelled zero. Store optional brand, barcode, original source, and per-value estimation/source status. Support editable, item-specific conversions for familiar units (slice, egg, cup, etc.); these are quantities of the same item, not separate database items. When recording, offer the **last five used quantities/units** plus a new quantity, as with water.

For temporary testing, **Settings → Copy approved foods** copies a complete, pasteable JSON export of approved records to the clipboard, including nutrient values, source details, barcodes, and familiar measures. Export is not a backup system. Earlier approved-food snapshots still merge once into **device-local prototype storage**, never silently into the connected shared database. In configured, authorised Firebase mode, the Food Item/Dish/Meal library instead loads and synchronises using Firestore snapshots and browser offline persistence. Existing device-local approved foods are kept separate and can be transferred **only by an explicit Settings action**, with cloud duplicates skipped, unchanged local copies retained, and unsafe linked recipes reported rather than guessed. Neither signing in nor enabling Firebase can silently overwrite local-approved items. Firebase remains unconfigured until project credentials and two memberships are supplied. Keep the user-approved snapshot separate from external food reference datasets. When the user supplies corrected label values for an already-approved food, distribute them in the approved-food snapshot and update only matching, previously incorrect local records on test devices; preserve separately edited records. The verified Barilla spaghetti values are 1,520 kJ and 358 kcal per 100 g; Ceres Organics quinoa/rice spaghetti is 1,590 kJ per 100 g. Approved Food Item changes affect future entries and recipes. A diary entry retains a **small snapshot of its recorded nutritional values**; old records need not be recalculated following a minor library correction unless the historical entry itself is edited. Daily totals can be cached for efficiency.

## 5. Dishes and Meals

Three searchable, independently favouriteable levels: **Food Item → Dish → Meal**. Any level may be recorded directly. Dishes can contain Food Items or other Dishes; Meals can contain Food Items, Dishes, or other Meals. Prevent circular references.

The **Make a Dish** and **Put together a Meal** forms search already-approved components, add them with editable quantities and applicable units (g, mL, defined familiar measures, or a whole nested Dish/Meal), and save only deliberately named recipes into the approved library. Each saved recipe has a calculated total size and nutrition per 100 g (and for the whole recipe), derived from current ingredient nutrition. Treat the mass of measured liquids as approximate where density is unknown. Stored component IDs preserve relationships so later food and recipe edits update linked derived totals. Prevent deletion of ingredients still referenced by recipes. **Capital Case is required for all saved names, including imported foods, dishes, and meals.**

A saved Dish/Meal has defined ingredient quantities. Users can add, omit, scale, or explicitly substitute components; do **not** impose automatic substitution rules. Calculate total Dish weight from ingredient quantities. From an existing Dish or Meal, **Save as variation** opens a new, separately named recipe with the existing components. It is **linked to its parent by default**, inheriting parent components and future edits except for changed quantities, removed ingredients, or explicit additions; users can deliberately detach it into a standalone recipe. Nested variations are supported. Dependency links are based on stable IDs and must reject circular references. Editing one recorded instance does not alter a shared template. New diary entries default to the saved recipe quantities, not the last recorded adjustment.

## 6. Calorie budget and trends

Initially calculate a **maintenance-calorie range** for current body weight, using an evidence-based resting-expenditure estimate plus tracked movement and exercise. Refine estimates cautiously over time from recorded intake and smoothed longer-term weight changes. Do not overfit normal weight fluctuations or presume an incomplete food diary is complete. Show uncertainty where it matters. The budget is calculated, **not manually overridden**.

Weight-loss targets, rates, target dates, and transitions can be developed later; values in the current sample charts are **fictional demonstrations**, not adopted personal targets.

## 7. Accounts, storage, and delivery

Use **Google sign-in and Firebase** with separate private personal records for the two users and a shared approved Food Item/Dish/Meal library. Firestore access is limited to **two manually provisioned member UIDs** (no self-enrolment); the Firestore rules make all personal subcollections accessible only to their owner. Shared approved-food changes use real-time listeners and persistent offline caching. Use Google popup login while hosted on GitHub Pages, with a clear signed-in/connected/cached/unauthorised state. Unconfigured or signed-out prototype editing stays local; local records must not be conflated with a signed-in user's records. Entries must work offline and sync when connected, without losing unsynced records. Provide export and **automatic recoverable off-device historical backups**, separate from normal live synchronisation, with almost no ongoing administration. Firestore native managed backups require billing: **no automatic-backup approach is yet agreed or implemented**. Validate a safe no-cost alternative before production use.

Keep the GitHub Pages build lightweight, with vanilla HTML/CSS/JavaScript and a manifest. After the first complete deployment, distribute updates as **patch ZIPs containing changed files only**; unchanged files remain in the existing GitHub Pages repository. Later, package as a signed Android app where necessary for automatic steps; test Health Connect on the intended Xiaomi phones **early**, not after the rest of development. Preserve device and account security and minimise permissions.

## 8. Prototype status and next work

**Current implementation (v0.1.039):** Existing chart, weight/water entry sheets, comprehensive read-only food references, barcode and OCR review, and Food Item/Dish/Meal composition remain. The chart and weight/water controls **still use fictional demo data**. A Firebase modular web adapter, editable public web-app config, Google popup sign-in, membership-restricted Firestore security rules, and a Settings connection/status interface are included. Once the project owner configures Firebase, publishes the rules, and provisions the two member UIDs, **the shared approved-food library can live-synchronise across the two accounts**. An explicit transfer action offers carefully checked, non-destructive migration of pre-existing device-local approved foods. Secure **private Firestore collections for weight, water, food diary, and exercise** have been defined, and the adapter can queue weight/water event documents; the existing entry sheets do **not yet use these paths**. Firestore uses persistent local browser caching where supported. A connection or synchronisation test against a live Firebase project has **not** yet been performed.

**Not yet production-ready:** Project-owner Firebase setup and live validation, reliable handling and testing of offline conflicts, actual private weight/water recording from the current entry controls, live graph/diary, nutrition-source gap research, exercise, Health Connect steps, adaptive calorie modelling, and **automatic recoverable off-device historical backups**. Native Firestore backups are not included on the no-cost plan. Local copies should be retained until migration and independent recovery are verified. Barcode lookup and OCR require continued tests on real NZ packaging.
**Next focus:** Complete and test Firebase project setup, memberships, and shared food synchronisation on both users' devices; then connect weight and water entry to owner-private records, replace the demo graph with live readouts, and develop diary recording. Independently validate a no-cost automatic recovery/backup approach. Continue OCR testing, especially `g`/`9` ambiguity and weak table rows. Preserve the agreed minimalist interface.
