# Jules Berne’s Pie Chart — Project Specification

## Mandatory development protocol

**This is the authoritative description of the current intended app, not a release diary.** At the start of **every** development request, the assistant must read the current `PROJECT_SPEC.md`, consider it alongside the user's request, and inspect the relevant existing code. Preserve established behaviour, reuse shared components, and identify contradictions or design choices requiring the user's agreement **before** making unrequested design changes. The user does not maintain or need to refer to this file.

Every delivered development patch must also update this file **in place**: replace superseded requirements, remove obsolete text and duplication, distinguish implemented from unverified behaviour, and maintain an internally consistent current specification. Do not append change logs or speculative features. Where the code, specification, and observed behaviour disagree, investigate and flag the discrepancy; never silently redefine the requirement. Only changed files belong in each patch ZIP.

**Baseline:** PieChart web app v0.1.110; separate Android Health Connect/ML Kit companion v0.2.016. **Status:** Current agreed product requirements and remaining development work are described below.

## 1. Purpose, architecture, and navigation

PieChart is a free, phone-first, practical nutrition, activity, water, and weight tracker, replacing Noom's recording functions but not its coaching. It is a lightweight GitHub Pages PWA (vanilla HTML/CSS/JavaScript and manifest), with Google sign-in/Firebase for data and a separate native Android companion providing Health Connect steps and experimental ML Kit nutrition-panel scanning. Keep everyday entry quick, the approved visual design consistent, and unnecessary controls out of the interface. During development the PWA uses **standalone**, not automatic fullscreen, display; do not lock orientation.

The **graph is the home screen**. Its compact header uses the approved right-facing pie-slice-on-nautical-chart artwork, the app name, a stroked binoculars history toggle immediately left of the settings cog, and the existing header height. Settings links, in order, to **Food library**, **Activity library**, **Details & Targets**, and **Account**. Account holds Google sign-in/out, connection status, manual food-library JSON copy/export, and source attribution. No demonstration CSV, fabricated measurement stream, or sample-history controls remain. The chart is populated only from personal records.

Double-tapping a home metric opens its appropriate daily dialog: **Weight → Add Weight; Steps → Today's Activities; Water → Today's Water; Calories/Fibre/Protein/Fats/Carbs → Today's Food**. A double-tap must not accidentally activate a control in the dialog it opens. Each daily dialog uses the same styling and a stable **90% of visible viewport** height, with one internal scrolling area, keyboard-aware resizing, accessible Close, and no jumping when results change. Editing an individual FDM or activity hides the daily date arrows until returning to the daily root.

## 2. Graph, readouts, and history

The eight default vertically stacked bands, in order, are **Weight, Steps, Water, Calories, Fibre, Protein, Fats, Carbs**. Each retains its approved colour, independent native-unit scale, fine per-day fades, and full-width separators. On the right, display the current/viewed value, applicable target or calorie range, and percentage if a valid target exists; a 12 px edge strip has a muted background and proportional coloured fill. Fats and Carbs have **no invented targets** and display whole grams without a space before `g` (e.g., `18g`) in their large readouts. Calories, Fibre, and Protein also display whole-number readings on the main screen; preserve the underlying recorded precision and any calculation precision. Weight percentage means **actual ÷ target**, not progress towards a loss. The Fats icon must look fatty/oily and differ from Water. Hide/show bands only via explicit eye controls; reorder them by long-pressing and dragging right-hand labels, without changing visibility on a plain tap.

The graph uses the same upright left-to-right layout in portrait and landscape. Initially show **100 calendar days**; horizontal gestures adjust the visible historical range, moving the time axis during the gesture and redrawing on release. Use monthly date marks, switching to years for long periods. All bands share one exact daily x-position per calendar date, even for missing/partial records; never compress dates or aggregate daily values into multi-day averages. The plotted range **ends yesterday**: today's incomplete values appear in the right-hand readings only and cannot influence any plotted scale or weight envelope. Show the true last date on the axis. A calorie column represents **kilocalories consumed**, not a percentage of budget; its percentage readout is relative to that day's calculated budget and therefore need not follow bar height.

All chart values come from real, owner-private Firestore records. Missing days and missing individual nutrients remain unknown and visually blank, not interpolated, silently set to zero, or populated from demonstration data. Signed-out mode contains an empty calendar chart, never another account's history. Imported Noom daily summaries live in `users/{uid}/dailyTotals/{YYYY-MM-DD}.noom` and supply dated steps, water, calories, protein, fibre, fats, carbohydrates, and original calorie budgets. The source-specific `noom` object can omit any measurement independently; never infer one field from another. Noom daily nutrient and water totals are added to subsequent PieChart serving/water recordings on the same date, rather than replaced; only Health Connect steps take precedence over imported Noom steps. The graph displays the total of the independent sources without rewriting the imported source. Within the owner's actual Noom import date range, original Noom calorie budgets are authoritative, including missing-budget days: do not substitute PieChart's calculated budgets anywhere in that period. Ordinary PieChart records entered with past dates outside the import period use PieChart's own budget calculation; the date of recording does not classify a record as a Noom import. The Noom period is established from the owner's earliest and latest imported Noom daily summary. Imported weight and activity events live as ordinary source-tagged personal records in `weightEntries` and `exerciseEntries` and obey existing history rules. Noom imports are numerical summaries: do not fabricate individual FDM servings from them. The historical Food and Water diaries show any imported Noom daily totals as labelled, non-editable aggregate rows alongside genuinely recorded PieChart entries. An imported Noom nutrient that is missing remains unknown; if a new PieChart serving supplies that nutrient, display its **known contribution** with a lower-bound (≥) indication, not a falsely complete Noom daily total. Do not replace a missing Noom value with zero or suppress the new serving.

Plot actual weigh-in dots without white outlines. A smooth filled weight high/low envelope uses genuine measurements from a **seven-calendar-day window**, including prior off-screen context; never invent missing weigh-ins. Draw the Weight target line only if it is already within the natural band scale; never stretch the scale to reach it. Draw applicable target lines and calorie-budget low/high lines with the existing coloured dashed treatment; **the bounds pass through the exact calculated value at each day centre**, without smoothing values across days. Interpolation between centres is visual only. Historical targets for Weight, Water, Steps, Protein, and Fibre use the member's current personal settings where no historical goal was actually recorded; do not fabricate past targets.

**History mode:** The binoculars toggle enters chart-date selection from today, then returns directly to today when tapped again. While selecting, a finger tap/drag previews a vertical date line and compact date popup; releasing chooses that date. The selected marker remains on the graph. Outside selection mode, chart taps do nothing, and range-zoom gestures work normally. The date popup has one-day back/forward arrows; there is no separate Past Date/Today button strip. Viewing a previous date gives the entire app/dialog a subdued lavender tone; advancing to today restores the normal tone. The plotted graph stays anchored to yesterday, rather than ending at the selected day. A selected date changes the readouts, diary lists, and destination of new entries, with no future weight leaking into earlier readings.

**Daily dialog navigation:** Today's Food, Water, and Activities each have **back/forward day arrows immediately left of Close**. They use the same shared selected-date state and history styling as the binoculars, moving exactly one calendar day, never past today or before available chart history. Their contents and recording dates update immediately. Returning to today restores live mode. The currently selected day is consistently respected by any nested editor, recording, or correction. All three recorded-entry lists (Food, Water, and Activities) display entries in chronological creation order, **oldest at the top, newest at the bottom**, including when viewing an earlier date. Imported Noom aggregate summary rows remain above later individual PieChart entries. This is a display order, not a change to stored timestamps or recent-shortcut ordering.

Exercise is represented on completed dates by **one small solid coral circle with a light outline near the foot of the Steps band**, whenever at least one valid activity is recorded for that date, even if no duration, distance, steps, or calorie estimate was supplied; it must not obscure the steps or target. A compact coral activity-count badge also appears by the Steps label for the viewed date, including today. No activity means no marker or badge; do not introduce a second step series or duration bars. Step columns and activity markers must refer to the stored, exact `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date; the plotted calendar-day slot and activity marker share its centre, including at the chart edges, without shifting/clamping markers horizontally. The chart must **never** be shifted forward to include Today to conceal a suspected date issue; verify source dates, their timezone, and chart alignment before changing any date handling.

## 3. Daily recording and shared form behaviour

**Weight:** An ordinary numeric kg input replaces the old ruler. Prefill the last **genuine** weigh-in on or before the viewed date (blank if none). Allow typing to 0.01 kg, show two decimals, and use 0.05 kg increments for press-and-hold scrubbing and native step controls; manual 0.01 kg entries remain valid. Save separate owner-private, timestamped weigh-in events with the local calendar date; the last genuine weigh-in of a day is the chart value. A historical date with a real weigh-in offers **Delete recorded weight** with confirmation, deleting only the most recent event on that date; an earlier event on that date can then be shown. Preserve irregular records and support corrections. Today's weight is shown in the home readout, not today's plot.

**Today's Food:** On a date containing a Noom import, show the available Noom nutritional totals as one read-only historical summary, followed by separate editable PieChart servings; do not duplicate Noom totals as invented foods. Show a centred large-metric-style **Calories remaining of maximum budget** above the existing shared Food Library search and scanner. Display recorded FDM servings for the viewed day with name, quantity/unit, and an unobtrusive **Delete** control on the right (confirmation); **no plus/minus controls**. Tap an entry to edit its own quantity and date, scaling the original recorded nutrition/water snapshot rather than pulling in changed library nutrients. Keep the **Favourites** list below the recorded entries, in its own gently bordered section, visually distinct from the recorded entries. On search focus, hide both lists and show nothing until typing begins; then show the shared typeahead results. Clearing the query and leaving search restores the daily lists. The Favourites heading has greater spacing above than the preceding list, uses **14 px** item-sized type, and has the same tight divider gap as Recorded today. New food servings record to the viewed day and return to **Today's Food** (or the selected historical day's food diary), never the standalone Food Library search, after recording. No compulsory meal-time categories.

**Today's Water:** Imported Noom daily water, when present, is a labelled, read-only historical summary; actual PieChart drinks remain separate editable/deletable entries, and the graph adds both amounts. Same daily-dialog styling and size. Show **litres remaining to today's water goal** (or unavailable/over-goal wording), then the selected day's recorded water-contributing food servings and legacy water entries in diary-row formatting. Below these show the smaller **Record Water** heading, five recent quick quantities, and a **New Amount** input. The **Record amount** button is a conventional, legible purple submit button within the shared sticky action-row component, never a separately sticky paper-coloured button. Choosing **New Amount** immediately reveals its form, focuses the amount, selects the existing value, and opens the numeric keyboard ready for typing. This deliberate action bypasses the normal double-tap-to-type requirement; the input otherwise supports common hold-to-scrub. Quick drinks are recorded as a Water **food-entry snapshot** using Water's configured contribution; old standalone `waterEntries` remain readable and counted, but are no longer written. Today's Water shows a confirmed **Delete** action beside every contributing FDM or legacy water entry. Deleting a contributing FDM deletes that **entire owner-private recorded Food Item/Dish/Meal snapshot**, removing its water, calories, and all other nutrient contributions from every daily total; it never deletes only the water component. Deleting a legacy water record removes that record's water only. Shortcut amounts stay private to the member. The shared Food Item named **Water** is required for shortcuts. Shortcuts use the same Food Item water attribution as normal servings (including explicitly configured percentages), preserve nutrient snapshots, and leave live chart refreshes to the incremental Firestore listener rather than forcing a full SVG redraw. Immediately after Firestore accepts a Water recording into the device's local write queue (including when offline), the Water metric shows a gentle 2.6-second bloom/glow without shifting its text or layout; do not wait for remote synchronisation. A synchronisation rejection must instead surface a separate visible warning, and validation failures never trigger the success animation. This animation is non-blocking and respects reduced-motion preferences.

**Today's Activities:** Same daily-dialog styling and size. Show **steps remaining to the viewed day's step goal**, its recorded activities, and access to the shared Activity Library's search/favourites and recording flow. In Today's Activities, show Recorded Today first, then Search Activities, then the personal Favourites box, in the same shared layout and with the same generous spacing between the recorded list and search as Today's Food. Searching hides the recorded and favourites sections; clearing and leaving search restores them. In the daily Activities view, place favourites within their own gently bordered section, distinct from recorded activities; the ordinary Activity Library search remains unboxed. Selecting any existing Activity from Today’s Activities search results or personal Favourites opens its prefilled private **Record activity** form for the selected date; it must never open shared-library editing or save the library instead of recording. Tapping an already recorded diary activity opens **Edit recorded activity** for that private entry. Selecting an Activity from Settings → Activity library opens the shared-library editor. Do not use the old duration-and-effort-only form.

For all three readouts, calculate the remaining amount from the viewed day's real entries and available goal/budget, say **over** rather than showing a negative remainder, and say unavailable when there is no valid target. On an unrecorded day, the dialog shows the entire goal/budget remaining as a recording convenience, **not** a claim that actual food intake or activity was zero. Omit those unrecorded values from historical averages, trends, and calculations requiring observed intake or activity. Historical dates use their own real records.

**Recorded diary rows:** Food, Water, and Activities share the same row, label/quantity layout, and Delete control styling. Deletion always confirms the full affected recording, and a water-contributing FDM deletes its entire food diary event, including all nutrients. Editing from Today's Water returns there after saving; editing from Today's Food returns there. Only entries with editable nutrition snapshots have an Edit action. The respective library result lists share a result renderer and search-input styling, while each domain retains its own results and available creation actions.

**Shared numeric and text inputs:** Use existing reusable field/popup components. When a numerical field is tapped/clicked once, it must not open a keyboard or text cursor; **double tap/click opens direct numeric typing**, selecting any existing value for replacement. An empty numeric field also allows double tap. **A stationary brief 190 ms press-and-hold activates shared scrubbing** with a subtle active indicator, without opening the keyboard. Ordinary swiping scrolls instead of changing values, and keyboard users can press Enter to unlock typing. The New Amount Water action explicitly opens the keyboard immediately, as described above. Text and other non-numeric inputs continue to select their contents on focus. Numerical touch scrubbing uses **one velocity-sensitive curve across fields**. Slow movement allows unit-appropriate precision (including **1 g** and **0.05 kg**); faster movement accelerates conservatively. A normal release stops; only a clearly fast flick coasts briefly with bounded, decaying momentum. Slowing, reversing, or a new touch restores fine control/stops inertia. Unit and precision determine the scale, not separate per-field gesture algorithms. Modal forms must keep Save, Record, Cancel, and other completion actions reachable while the phone keyboard is open, with a visible-height-constrained sheet, independently scrolling form content, and sticky action rows where appropriate. Native Android keyboard operation remains to be phone-verified.

## 4. Food Library, FDMs, and recipes

**FDM** means Food Item, Dish, or Meal. All authorised accounts share the *definitions*, but each member has **private, cross-device favourites** and their own recording history. An unchanged Firestore notification must never clear a loaded shared Food/Dish/Meal library; clear the displayed library only on sign-out, account change, or genuine loss of authorisation. Neither favourites nor other metadata refreshes may wipe the local library view. Approved library items are individually reviewed and intentionally saved; do not bulk-seed from references, temporary local snapshots, or generic sample foods. Old temporary approved-food test data are retired; preserve all genuine live shared Food Items and recipes. Names are **Capital Case** in storage and display. Do not label items “approved” in the interface.

Use **one Food Library search/scan/typeahead controller** wherever FDMs are chosen, including diary search and ingredient selection. In Today's Food and Today's Activities, use the same Recorded → Search → personal Favourites arrangement and shared markup/spacing helper, allowing the domain-specific search controls to differ. Leave a clear 26 px gap between the bottom of the recorded section and the search row; do not apply that gap to the standalone libraries. Search focus hides recorded entries and favourites until the query is cleared and focus leaves. In Today's Food, the search row also includes barcode Scan. Keep the ordinary Food Library search at the top when not in the diary. Before searching, show only relevant personal favourites; do not automatically suggest recent but non-favourite FDMs or Activities. When typing, show matching items in this order: **Favourites → other matching saved FDMs → Add new Food Item → Make a Dish → Put together a Meal → NZ reference → Australian reference → USA/USDA reference**. Creation actions are ordinary result rows only while searching, not separate buttons. Within each group, rank by search relevance, then name, without recent-use ranking. Search may use food names or barcode numbers. **After a camera barcode scan, if exactly one saved shared Food Item matches the scanned code, open that existing library item immediately in the current selection/editor workflow; do not query Open Food Facts or require an extra search-result tap.** If no exact match or multiple matches exist, retain normal barcode results and manual choice. Keep tags (Food Item, Dish, Meal) visible, with distinct green/orange/purple badges; source-only results look lighter and indicate provenance. No extra provider panels/headings or explanation cards. Keep input text at least 16 px without an added external focus outline; results scroll without visible scrollbar/gutter. Place the scanner preview at the top of the library card. On saving a **new** Food Item, Dish, or Meal, including **Save as new**, open **Record serving** for the newly saved FDM; cancelling recording returns to the reset library/daily search. Updating an existing FDM returns to the reset/refocused search. Saving an ingredient returns to the unfinished recipe with all unsaved details preserved.

Bundle locally searchable **read-only** Food Library lookup indexes outside the shared library: **NZ Concise Food Composition Tables 14th edition (2021), 1,278 records; Australian Food Composition Database Release 3, 1,588; USDA Foundation Foods October 2026, 424** (3,290 total), in that priority order. The **separate fibre estimator**, loaded **only when requested**, uses the original NZ FOODfiles 2024 food types and fibre records (2,857 foods), AFCD's official AUSNUT hierarchical group codes, and USDA reference foods. Published fibre, density, and classification source values remain unchanged; PieChart's food-type crosswalk and estimates are explicitly derived. NZ FOODfiles provides 2,855 FIBTG records and 30 FIBTLC records, covering 2,857 foods (some overlap). Usable density records exist for 2,506 foods, but conversion is permitted only where appropriate to the specific food and measures. Retain attribution, date, licence notices, and original references; incomplete source nutrients remain missing, not zero. The NZ live database has no verified public search API. For barcodes use **Open Food Facts only** at present; its nutrition is not guaranteed reliable. Missing energy must remain missing for user review and input: do not infer it from protein, fat, carbohydrates, or fibre. Conversion between provided kJ and kcal is permitted, with source provenance retained. A failed network lookup must not be called a missing product: try the official Open Food Facts v2 and v3 product API routes (without retry storms during rate limiting), display the failure reason, and offer a direct Open Food Facts product-page link/manual label entry when the service is inaccessible; do not substitute another provider. Prefer an English product name, else a barcode placeholder and manual naming. FoodSwitch, USDA Branded barcode lookups, FSANZ branded foods, and GS1 are **not integrated or authorised substitute providers**. Never automatically add external food records to the shared library.

External/reference/barcode items open the **same editable Food Item review**, titled simply **Food Item**, with an explicit **Add to library** action. The editor has one Food name field and no separate Brand field: when a barcode lookup includes a brand, prefix it to the Food name with a space, preserving recognisable brand capitalization. Previously saved records containing a separate brand remain legible and editable; saving folds that brand into the name. **Do not show or require an editable Source field.** Retain existing/imported source provenance internally and keep the original-source link for recognised references where available; manual entries record internal manual provenance automatically. Place the personal Favourite toggle and Counts towards water intake on one row immediately below Food name, using the same checkbox sizing, font, spacing, and accent as the equivalent Dish, Meal, and Activity editor toggles. Place Barcode and **Values per 100** on the next two-column row. The latter selector displays **Grams** and **Millilitres**, while retaining stored g/mL units. Display five legible, editable nutrient cards in the main screen's colours: Energy (orange), Protein (green), Fat (pink), Carbs (blue), and Fibre (purple). **At all screen widths, show all five cards in one horizontal row in the exact order Energy, Protein, Fat, Carbs, Fibre, as in the user-supplied Food Item mock-up.** Energy stacks kJ above Cal within its card; the other cards display large inline numbers with their gram units. Use compact, borderless editable numeric fields integrated visually into the cards (retaining the common double-tap-to-type and hold-to-scrub gestures). Retain a compact Fibre **Estimate** button (accessible as **Provide fibre estimate**) inside the Fibre card, vertically centred as part of the value-and-button group rather than anchored to the bottom; its longer explanation and source appear **below** the cards. All five cards must share consistent centred alignment, text colour, typography, spacing, and restrained 6 px coloured top borders, without an artificial empty third row or wide label-to-value gap. Show Cal values rounded to whole numbers without discarding their precise editable values or changing kJ/Cal synchronisation. Use this **same component** for existing and new Food Item editors and for the calculated, read-only nutrient summary in Dish and Meal editors. Recipe cards show **whole-recipe totals**, with calculated recipe weight and water shown in a quiet line beneath, not an unannounced conversion to per-100 values. Allow no clipped or overlapping numbers or horizontal overflow. Do not redesign or stack the cards at phone widths without approval. Retain the fibre estimate control and scanner functionality. Sticky Back/Add to library actions must have no redundant gap underneath and remain reachable with the phone keyboard open. **Scan nutrition panel** is a separate action in this existing Food Item form, distinct from the PWA's barcode scanner. The agreed Android production path launches the installed PieChart Companion directly into its on-device ML Kit camera scanner and returns to the same unsaved Food Item form with the final per-100 g/mL readings for review. This is an **app-to-app transition**, not a native camera embedded inside Chrome; both screens should share PieChart's visual language so the transition feels integrated. Scanner-internal review and prompted individual nutrient rescanning take place **before** returning. PieChart alone handles manual corrections, energy conversion, validation, and saving; no values are automatically saved.

The main app must preserve the entire unsaved Food Item draft, navigation context, and selected date across the handoff, even if Android opens a fresh PWA instance. It accepts only the response for the outstanding scan request; cancellation, no result, duplicate returns, and a missing companion must not lose or overwrite the draft. A completed scan supplies **five explicit fields**, with each verified per-100 g/mL candidate carrying original printed numeric text, original unit, and status **confirmed**, **provisional**, or **not_found**. **All recognised values, including provisional readings, return to PieChart** for review; provisional fields are visibly marked **check** and retain normal editable inputs. Missing/unusable fields remain blank; if the per-100 heading is unverified, no numbers may be assigned as per-100 values. PieChart converts energy between kJ/kcal correctly and never saves scanned data automatically. The user must review and correct provisional values before approving the Food Item. Preserve an Undo/Restore previous nutrient values action. No scanned data is saved automatically; scanner results must never trigger the dummy-test Save/Record block. Never substitute per-serving figures. No nutrition images or OCR text are sent to Firebase.

**Integrated scanner status:** The current PWA and companion v0.2.016 camera handoff are implemented in source but **not yet phone-tested together**. The earlier Stage 1 test using five fixed dummy values successfully restored the Food Item draft in v0.1.079. The native scanner launches directly with **Cancel** or **Done**. Done returns confirmed **and provisional** numeric readings for an independently verified per-100 column, with field-level confidence status; Cancel never changes the draft. All provisional entries in PieChart are visibly identified for review. The original short-lived local draft, session-bound callback validation, Firebase-ready restoration, and fallback handling remain. A legacy `test` status from an old companion is still recognised and blocked from saving. Android may open a new browser tab; reliably reusing the original PWA tab is **not yet demonstrated**.

The existing browser-based photograph reader (including manual per-100 column selection) remains available on Android as **Use photograph instead**, and remains the default on other platforms. Its OCR accuracy has been inadequate in user testing, but it must not be silently deleted. The companion scanner is **natively coded to match the approved Food Item review/dialogue mockup**, not dynamically rendered from the PWA CSS: the same cream sheet, rounded frame, navy title, muted details, nutrient colours, five compact rows, and lavender Done / grey Cancel buttons. There is no separate-companion branding or instruction paragraph at the top. Values are normal-weight until genuinely confirmed, then bold. Tools such as debug, photograph, sample capture, and copy OCR are in a secondary options menu; screenshot remains accessible in the title bar.

**Fibre estimation:** A small **Provide estimate** control next to fibre classifies the food by **type, subtype, and relevant preparation**, rather than searching for similar protein, fat, carbohydrate, or calorie values (which must not influence confidence). Use food-group definitions from the Australian AUSNUT hierarchy and descriptive NZ FOODfiles fields, with a curated crosswalk for fibre-relevant subclasses and cautious source-name fallback when a specific food type is not in the crosswalk. From suitable foods in that group, offer the **median** fibre value and show reference count, **observed minimum/maximum**, actual source-food names, and the amount of variation. Let the user change the food type or refine cooking/processing. If the type cannot be inferred, ask for a food-group choice rather than manufacturing a value. For per 100 mL, use source-native values or documented density measurements, never assume 1 g/mL. **Do not write a proposed value until the user presses Use this estimate.** Persist its food type, observation range, representative sources, and method as an estimate; edits to its food name (including brand prefix), base unit, or fibre invalidate the former attribution. Wide spreads and single-source estimates should be flagged as limited evidence. This function remains independent of the approved/shared Food Library and does not auto-add reference foods. The v0.1.082 algorithm is initially source/test-verified but awaits browser and real-food validation.

**Water from foods:** Quick Water uses the existing shared Food Item named Water (whose nutrient basis must be per 100 mL), storing the same nutrient and attributed-water snapshot that the ordinary Food Item Record serving screen would store; it never overrides an explicitly configured 0–100% hydration contribution. The optional **Counts towards water intake** control sits directly below Food name, beside Favourite, defaults off except Water (100% for Water without an explicit setting), and chooses 0–100% of the residual per-100 mass after protein, fat, carbohydrate, and fibre. Avoid double-subtracting fibre if carbohydrates already include it (USDA). Clamp residual at zero and use approximate 1 g/mL density for liquids where unknown. Only explicitly flagged foods contribute; nested recipes contribute their ingredient water proportionately and exactly once. Historical food entries retain a nutrient **and water snapshot**, so later library changes do not rewrite the diary retrospectively. Keep each new serving’s original, higher-precision quantity, nutrients, and attributed-water basis in a private `snapshotBasis` field. Repeated quantity edits must rescale from that original basis, never the last rounded result; old entries without a basis acquire one from their saved values on first edit. Round only displayed or saved derived totals, not the preserved basis.

**Recipes:** Dishes can contain Food Items or Dishes; Meals can contain Food Items, Dishes, or Meals, with **no circular dependency**. Reuse the same library search and Food Item editor when adding ingredients; ingredient quantities use g, mL, valid familiar measures, or whole nested dishes/meals. Show ingredients **above** Add to Dish/Meal, with touch-friendly drag handles; save their explicit order, including linked variations. Derived recipe size, per-100 and full-recipe nutrients/water are calculated from its components; measured liquid mass may be approximate. Prevent deletion of a referenced ingredient. Linked recipes use stable IDs so source updates propagate according to the saved variation rules; do not impose automatic substitutions.

**Save and record:** Every FDM editor has a prominent solid **Record serving of [name]** button at the top, recording the current edited draft to the owner-private diary without changing the shared definition. When Save is pressed **after changing an existing FDM**, show a real popup, not a form extension, with exactly: **Record serving with these values → Update original [FDM type] → Save as new [FDM type]**. Closing or Escape keeps the edited draft; no Keep editing button. Save as new prompts for a unique name **within the same popup**, prefilled with the original name and cursor at its end; no separate variation form and no silently overwritten original. Food variations are distinct Food Items. Dish/Meal variations are linked to the edited parent by default, inherit later parent changes except explicit overrides, and can be detached. The link control reads **Update details when [parent name] is updated**. Nested variations are supported without circular links. New diary entries use saved template quantities, not the last instance's edits. Saving a new FDM proceeds immediately to its editable Record serving screen; an update to an existing definition does not force recording.

## 5. Activity Library and step reconciliation

The **Activity Library is one shared library**, just like FDM definitions; authorised members can create/edit saved named **instances** (e.g., a 7.66 km walk and a 10.5 km walk are separate entries), but their favourites and recorded activities are private. There are no Activity equivalents of Dishes/Meals. Reuse the FDM editor's field, button, search/favourites, Save popup, variation naming, and general layout conventions. The Activity editor uses the same name-first layout and **Favourite** checkbox immediately below the name; favourites belong only to the signed-in account. The shared Activity definition never shows a recording date or calculated expenditure. The recording form shows the selected date and expenditure preview. Save validation must match the numeric ranges offered by its fields, including fractional minutes from 0.1 upward. A newly queued library item must become searchable and, when marked, visible in personal favourites without waiting for server round-trip; rejected writes must produce a visible error. A saved activity can define an activity type and relevant **time, distance, step count, or fallback subjective effort**. The saved details are defaults only: recording may adjust them, save a new named instance, or update the original. **Creating a new Activity follows new Food Items:** first save the shared library definition, then open a separate, editable Record activity step prefilled with its measurements. Saving the definition does not record anything. Record requires explicit confirmation and returns to the selected day’s Activities diary; Cancel returns to the Activity Library without adding a diary record. Editing an existing library definition does not automatically start recording. Date, calculated expenditure, and step-overlap decision belong to the **private recording**, not to the shared definition.

Calculate **one** activity expenditure estimate from the most informative measured inputs, never sum time-, distance-, and step-based estimates. For walking/running/hiking, distance + time yields speed/pace; manually entered steps are treated as accurate. Steps without distance yield an explicitly estimated distance from stride length, used only for activity energy. Duration- or distance-only activities must **not** invent step counts or increase the Steps total; only an explicitly measured/manually entered count may do that. For supported cycling/swimming use distance/time to estimate intensity where appropriate. Distance-only or steps-only energy estimates are provisional. Subjective Light/Moderate/Vigorous effort is a fallback **only when objective inputs cannot determine intensity**, especially duration-only records. Require at least one useful measurement, but no separately entered calories burned.

**After pressing Record**, if an activity has an explicitly entered step count, show a modal asking **“Are these steps additional to those recorded by Health Connect?”** The last Yes/No answer for that member **on that device** is preselected next time; an edited recording uses its own saved answer. The user can change it before recording. Never show this as an editor field or save the answer to the shared library. **Yes** adds these steps to the displayed daily total **alongside**, not inside, Health Connect's unchanged measured daily total. **No** means they are already included. Only explicitly entered step counts affect the step display. Older records with previously generated step counts must remain readable, but their `stepOrigin: estimated` counts must not be added to measured steps or represented as genuine measurement. Do not guess overlap, assume an unknown Health Connect day means zero measured steps, or add activity calories twice. Tapping a recorded activity opens its private recorded-instance editor, prefilled with the original date and measurements; Save changes updates the existing recording by ID and never modifies the shared Activity Library definition or personal favourites. Its form uses the recording controls, not library Save/variation/deletion actions. Private activity entries remain editable/deletable and can be backdated. Preserve readability of old activity records.

## 6. Personal details and targets

Settings → **Details & Targets** contains optional **date of birth, height (cm), and sex for the energy equation**, then optional **Weight (kg), Water (litres), Steps, Protein (g), and Fibre (g)** goals. **Goal intensity** sits immediately below Weight and is Gentle, Moderate (default), or Strong, controlling the intended pace of weight change, **not** the width of the calorie range. Do not introduce an independent calorie target, range-width option, usual-activity-level selector, or fat/carbohydrate goals. Personal details and goals remain genuinely unset when blank.

Store details and goals together in owner-private `users/{uid}/settings/targets`, synchronised only between that member's devices. Do not enable editing until the stored settings have loaded, to avoid overwriting pending/offline data with blanks. **Save** returns to Settings; **Back** discards edits. The calculator may show a range only when date of birth, height, sex-for-equation, target weight, and an applicable genuine current or historical weight are available. Historical imported Noom budgets remain independent of these conditions. The Weight percent readout keeps the **actual ÷ target** convention.

## 7. Calorie budget and energy model

Provide a provisional **daily calorie-intake target range**, not a deadline or precise expenditure measurement, based on the current weight goal. Show this in the Calories readout and, on eligible completed days, as the original translucent range with exact dashed low/high boundaries. No manual calorie-budget override or assumed fat/carbohydrate targets; original imported Noom budgets are historical observations, not editable overrides.

`calorie-budget.js` implements **2023 National Academies adult (19+) EER equations** using the published inactive/low-active sex-specific coefficients, actual age, height, and applicable weight. Use the **most recent owner-private weigh-in on or before each date**, never a future measurement; missing weigh-ins are not interpolated. For ordinary PieChart dates, calculate the budget from personal details and available steps and activities. For **Noom-imported dates**, display the **original Noom daily budget bounds** (`calorieBudgetLowKcal`/`calorieBudgetHighKcal`) where supplied, or the single `calorieBudgetKcal` as a single target if no bounds exist. Do not recalculate, average, or alter these imported historical figures, even if they differ from today's PieChart targets. If Noom has no budget for that date, leave that budget missing rather than manufacturing one. These source-recorded historical targets do not create a user-editable manual daily budget.

Goal-intensity adjustments relative to estimated maintenance are: **loss** Gentle 5–10%, Moderate 10–15%, Strong 15–20% below; **gain** Gentle 2–5%, Moderate 5–8%, Strong 8–11% above; **at goal** ±2.5%. Moderate is the default. Range *width* arises independently from plausible activity variation. Determine whether at goal using the median of at least **three applicable weigh-ins in 14 days** within **0.25 kg**, otherwise the latest; use the latest for EER itself. No target date or linear kcal/kg weight-loss prediction.

EER already includes resting expenditure, routine activity, and digestion: never add resting metabolism or thermic effect again. Add only extra step energy above a **3,000 steps/day** heuristic baseline, approximately **0.0005 kcal/kg/metre** using estimated stride **0.415 × height(m)**; these are approximations, not exact individual costs. Use up to **14 completed step days in the previous 21 days**, requiring at least **three** for a reliable 20th/50th/80th-percentile distribution. Only **today's** budget may use that recent distribution to project the unobserved portion of today (including when no steps have been received yet); never project steps from adjacent days into any completed historical date. On historical dates, count only steps actually recorded for that date (including a partial total if present), plus the recorded activities associated with that date. When a historical date has no step record, do not infer a count from other days; use the inactive baseline with the existing conservative inactive-to-low-active uncertainty range, rather than inventing step measurements. If today's private steps are completely absent, retain the conservative inactive-to-low-active maintenance range, not invented counts. Prior-day exercise is never projected into today. Bounds reflect maintenance uncertainty and goal-intensity edges and are rounded **outwards to the nearest 25 kcal**.

Recorded activities add **net** expenditure above rest (about `(MET − 1) × kg × minutes / 60`) with their own day-specific MET estimate. For step-overlapping activities, subtract step energy already counted in the reconciled step total before adding the single activity estimate. When an activity is step-based but has no recorded steps, treat potential overlap conservatively rather than manufacturing a step count. Preserve the existing activity-energy model until an alternative is expressly decided. The calorie engine accepts date-specific `stepDays` and `activities` inputs from private Firestore listeners; source `dailyTotals` are never overwritten by activity totals. No routine double-counting, false assumption of a complete food diary, or invented exercise. The model remains provisional and uncalibrated against personal trends.

**Safety bounds:** Automatic budgets cover adults **19–110** with sufficient personal details; pregnancy/breastfeeding and other circumstances needing tailored advice are not yet screened. Suppress loss targets when current or target BMI is below **18.5**, do not automatically offer underweight maintenance, and suppress the **entire** calorie range if its low bound is below **1,200 kcal/day**, requesting review rather than silently flooring it. These guards do not establish individual safety or adequacy. Future calibration must use sustained evidence and validated dynamic models, not fixed kcal/kg assumptions.

**Model references:** [NASEM adult EER coefficients](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-nutrition/healthy-eating/dietary-reference-intakes/tables/equations-estimate-energy-requirement.html); [2024 Adult Compendium](https://pacompendium.com/adult-compendium/); [NASEM activity limits](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK591020/); [ACSM walking equation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7896743/); [NICE guidance](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng246/chapter/Physical-activity-and-diet). [NIH Body Weight Planner](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/weight-management/body-weight-planner) is a future-model reference, not an implemented component.
