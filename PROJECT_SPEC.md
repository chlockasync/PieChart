# Jules Berne’s Pie Chart — Project Specification

**Status:** Agreed product requirements, with implementation status noted separately below.  
**Current prototype:** v0.1.044  
**Project/package name:** `PieChart`

## 1. Purpose and approach

A free, phone-first personal nutrition, activity, and weight tracker, designed primarily for everyday use by one person, with a second user able to test and maintain it. It replaces the practical tracking functions of Noom, not its coaching or educational content. Everyday recording should be quick; the app should not require routine database administration.

Develop the web app on GitHub Pages first, then provide Android integration where browser capabilities are insufficient. Keep the interface lean, touch-first, and consistent with the existing approved design. Do not introduce dashboards, controls, or complexity without a clear need.

## 2. Home screen and charts

**The home screen is the graph**, with a compact title bar featuring the approved right-facing meat-pie slice on a nautical chart, the name **Jules Berne’s Pie Chart**, and settings. The installed-app icon uses the same artwork. Enlarge the title, logo, and settings control to use available room **within the existing title-bar height**, without reducing graph space. The Fats metric uses a visibly fatty/oily icon, distinct from Water.

Eight independent, vertically stacked chart bands appear by default, in this order: **Weight, Steps, Water, Calories, Fibre, Protein, Fats, Carbs**. Each has its own native-unit vertical scale, a distinct existing colour, fine daily marks with short downward fades, and thin full-width separators. The integrated right-hand label column shows the metric, actual reading, applicable target/budget, and a prominent percentage when a valid target exists. The far-right, 12 px colour strip has a muted full-height background and a saturated fill proportional to target achievement. Do **not** invent fat or carbohydrate targets. Weight percentage represents actual weight divided by target weight, not weight-loss progress.

When signed into an authorised Firebase account, the chart uses **that account’s live private records from its first actual recorded entry onwards**. Only earlier dates may display the bundled CSV as labelled historical sample data; on and after the first live record, values for unrecorded metrics remain missing. No sample entry may overwrite a live day. The CSV is a public GitHub Pages file, and its historical data is visible to both members; it is not private, per-account history. When signed out, the sample chart remains available as a labelled prototype. The graph plots **individual daily records at every date range**, never aggregated multi-day averages. Initially show 100 days of the available history. Horizontal swipes reveal more or less historical data; the time axis moves during gestures, while the graph redraws on release. The axis marks actual calendar months, switching to year markers for long spans. The last completed day’s date belongs directly beneath its final plotted day column. Today's incomplete values appear in the right-hand readouts, **not** as a misleading partially completed chart column. There is no period selector.

Weight is shown using **actual weigh-in dots without white outlines** and a smooth filled high/low envelope derived from a seven-calendar-day window of genuine measurements, including context preceding the displayed period. Never invent missed weigh-ins. When the Weight target falls inside the existing visible weight-band scale, draw its coloured dashed target line like the other metrics, continuing the signed-in user’s current target through their visible earlier CSV history without adopting fictional CSV weight goals; **never expand the weight scale merely to display a distant target**. Calorie ranges and valid goals use the existing coloured dashed styling. **Do not show any popup or tooltip when tapping a chart day**; retain swiping/zooming, and revisit day interaction later. Preserve the currently approved band geometry and graph treatment unless a change is specifically requested.

Both portrait and landscape use the **same upright, left-to-right chart**, scaled to fit. Users can hide/show individual bands with explicit eye controls and reorder them by long-pressing and dragging their right-hand labels. A plain label tap must not accidentally change visibility. The installed PWA should request fullscreen without locking orientation.

## 3. Recording and daily history

- **Weight:** Double-tap its chart label to open the existing spacious entry sheet. Weigh-ins are stored as separate owner-private timestamped Firestore events with their local calendar date; the last actual weigh-in on a date determines that date’s chart value. Today’s reading appears immediately in the metric label and joins the plotted history on the following day. The daily graph never invents weigh-ins. Historical correction/deletion is still required. A single horizontal, speed-sensitive ruler starts from the previous measurement; slow swipes allow fine movement and the selected reading **snaps to 0.05 kg increments** (matching the scales), while fast swipes still cover more ground with momentum. Preserve all existing ruler markings and drawing geometry. The fixed full reading sits directly beneath the pointer. The screen has **Record** and **Cancel** only. Preserve irregular weigh-in history and allow corrections/deletions.
- **Water:** Water is an ordinary shared Food Item named exactly **Water** (normally per 100 mL). Every Food Item may explicitly contribute towards the water total via an optional **Counts towards water intake** control in the Food Item review/editor immediately below **Scan nutrition panel**. Off by default (zero water), except Water defaults to 100% for existing items without the setting and for newly named Water. When enabled, the user chooses **0–100% of the remaining 100 g/100 mL after subtracting protein, fat, carbohydrate, and fibre**, treated as an approximate water-mL contribution per 100 g/mL (1 g/mL density assumption for volume units). Avoid subtracting fibre twice where the source reports total carbohydrate inclusive of fibre (notably USDA). Clamp negative remaining mass to zero. Ordinary solid foods do not silently count towards water. The existing five recent amounts and New amount interface remains; shortcut drinks record a `foodEntries` snapshot for Water using its configured contribution, not always the full input volume. When any Food Item is included in a Dish or Meal, its water amount contributes proportionally, even through nested recipes and scaling, without double counting. Older standalone `waterEntries` remain counted for backwards compatibility. Water must exist in the shared library for the shortcut. Recent choices do not mix users.
- **Interaction:** A chart-label double-tap opens one intended entry sheet; neither tap may activate an action within the newly opened sheet.
- **Food:** A minimal private recording path is available from a saved Food Item, Dish, or Meal: **Record serving** with quantity and base/whole/familiar unit. Record a nutrient snapshot and calculated Water contribution in `foodEntries`; the current-day chart labels include logged nutrients and water. This is an interim recording route, not the final quick-entry flow or chronological diary. Nutrition-metric double taps still open the library, not a diary. Later recording should favour relevant saved items and require minimal taps.
- **Exercise:** Record activity type, duration/time, and intensity when relevant; calculate estimated energy use, rather than requiring manually supplied calories burned. Support favourite activities. Avoid double-counting exercise and steps.
- **Diary:** Recorded intake appears as a straightforward chronological list. No compulsory breakfast/lunch/dinner categories or meal times. Historical entries can be corrected.

**Personal targets:** Settings → **Personal targets** opens one page containing optional Weight (kg), Water (litres), Steps, Protein (g), and Fibre (g) targets. Targets are stored only under the signed-in user’s Firestore settings, synchronised across that user’s devices, and never shared with the other account. Empty fields explicitly mean no target; never inherit fictional CSV goals. Targets are editable only when account data has loaded, preventing an offline uninitialised device from silently overwriting saved values. Saving valid targets closes the Targets sheet and returns to the chart; **Back** returns to Settings without saving. Show actual/target percentages only where a target is set, and preserve the approved Weight actual ÷ target convention. No manual calorie-budget override and no invented fat/carbohydrate targets.

Steps must be obtained automatically from a compatible Android source (planned Health Connect), with **no manual step-entry screen** and no accessory requirement.

## 4. Approved food library

The approved library contains **only food records individually reviewed and explicitly approved by the users**. It must never be prepopulated with generic sample foods or bulk imports from nutritional reference datasets. Historical temporary food snapshots are retired; do not seed the new shared library from them.

The Food Library screen contains **one typeahead input for food names or barcode numbers, a Scan button, and one results list inside the dialog’s single scrollable pane**. Searching does not change the form scale: the input is at least 16 px and does not acquire an extra external focus outline. Do not add explanatory paragraphs, provider sections, separate result headings, cards, or additional action panels. Display matching library items first, with **Food Item, Dish, or Meal** type badges only; never call those items “approved” anywhere in the interface. External suggestions have visibly lighter name text and are marked by source (NZ, Australian, USDA, barcode), indicating they are not yet part of the library. A manual “Add new Food Item” row appears once a search begins, immediately below library matches and above every external source result. Result rows must fit on-screen, **scrolling without a visible scrollbar or a reserved scrollbar gutter, while keeping item-type tags fully visible**. Do not solve this by unnecessary text wrapping or wasting the gap between name and tag. The barcode scanner preview sits at the **top** of the Food Library card while active. Show approved records as **Food Item**, **Dish**, or **Meal**, using distinct colours already present in the graph (green, orange, and purple). The list ends with **Make a Dish** and **Put together a Meal** actions. **After the user types any search**, include a normal **Add new Food Item** result row immediately below library matches, ahead of all external results (including exact barcode and source matches). Prepopulate the new food name from the search text (or the barcode field for a searched barcode); use the standard editable review/approval form. Do not display this row before searching or add a permanently visible Create button. Search results and stored Food Item, Dish, and Meal names use **Capital Case**, never full capitals or arbitrary source casing. All modal screens (Settings, Account, Targets, food search, Food Item review, and Dish/Meal editing) must remain navigable with an on-screen keyboard. The dialog must resize to the actual visible viewport and use one scrollable content area, with active inputs kept in view and the dialog title/close control accessible. The NZ Food Composition Database is the primary reference for ordinary foods. Bundle **read-only, locally searchable reference indexes** from the NZ Concise Food Composition Tables 14th edition (2021, **1,278** distinct per-100 g records), Australian Food Composition Database Release 3 (**1,588**, including per-100 mL liquids), and USDA Foundation Foods October 2026 (**424**) in that order. All **3,290** records remain outside the approved library; users need not download or import them. Each source must remain visually identifiable and retain attribution, date, and original source references. The live NZ online database may be newer, but has no verified supported public search API.

For packaged foods, support typing or scanning barcodes and prefill name, brand, barcode, and straightforward pack-size conversions when available. Label any barcode data source (currently Open Food Facts as an expressly identified public fallback), and emphasise that **successful barcode identification does not guarantee correct nutrition**. Source values may be incomplete, outdated, or wrongly transcribed; provide editable nutrition fields and require checking and explicit approval. FoodSwitch and GS1 data are not free programmatic sources unless access is verified. No reference or barcode source can populate the approved library automatically. Australian data attribution and licence notices must be maintained; USDA Foundation data are public domain. Preserve incomplete USDA nutrients as missing, never as implicit zero.

Selecting any external suggestion opens an editable review form. **Only an explicit “Add to library” save action** adds an external item to the shared library; this internal approval gate must not appear as an “approved” label in the interface. Product-label capture is a two-step option: scan a barcode to identify a packaged product, then use **Scan nutrition panel** on its review form to photograph or choose a label image. On-device OCR searches the photograph for **nutrient names first**; uses their positions to locate the panel and, where possible, rereads that region in table mode. It identifies the relevant **per-100 g/mL column by its heading and horizontal position**, then reads the intersections of nutrient rows with that column, regardless of nutrient order or intervening extra nutrients. If the heading is unclear but nutrient rows are recognisable, show the photograph and allow the user to tap the per-100 column. Never assume the last column or OCR text sequence is correct. It must not silently accept ambiguous values (e.g. an OCR `g` misread as a trailing `9`); unresolved values remain editable and require manual checking. The app never saves serving-column figures as per-100 values. The review form highlights values read from the photo, leaves unclear/missing fields for manual correction, and requires the user to verify the actual label before approving. The photo is not uploaded to any server or retained in the database; initial OCR model files may require an internet connection. Provide source information, allow manually entering a specific Food Item, and never silently populate the library or replace NZ name-search data with a different provider.

Every approved Food Item must have **energy, protein, carbohydrates, fats, and fibre**, normally per **100 g or 100 mL**. The review form is minimal and arranges nutrients **one per row in NZ food-label order**: energy, protein, fat, carbohydrate, and fibre. Energy has adjacent kJ and kcal inputs on the same row; each other nutrient has one numeric input with g units. Avoid explanatory per-field captions and clutter. Editing either energy field immediately updates the other using **1 kcal = 4.184 kJ**. Editing either energy input must immediately cross-calculate the other. Preserve legitimate small differences between both values printed on a manufacturer label; when external energy figures disagree materially (over 2%), use their kJ value to derive kcal unless the user explicitly corrects them. A missing calorie value is derived from kJ. Display **all nutrients and energy values with a maximum of two decimal places**, irrespective of source-database precision, and round approved nutritional values to at most two decimal places on save. Retain the unmodified external reference datasets; do not silently replace verified product-label figures or introduce explanatory field clutter. Missing figures require a reasonable, identified estimate rather than omission or an unlabelled zero. Store optional brand, barcode, original source, and per-value estimation/source status. Support editable, item-specific conversions for familiar units (slice, egg, cup, etc.); these are quantities of the same item, not separate database items. When recording, offer the **last five used quantities/units** plus a new quantity, as with water.

**Shared-library state:** Two Google accounts are authorised in the separate PieChart Firebase project, and the user verified that two Food Items created on the phone appeared on the computer. Start the shared approved library fresh. Earlier temporary approved-food snapshots are **retired** and are never automatically seeded or uploaded; existing old local copies, if any, are not deleted. The app may retain its prior device-local records without silently merging them into the authorised cloud library. Food Items, Dishes, and Meals remain shared; personal records and settings remain private to each member. Account → Copy food library produces a manual JSON export, **not** a recoverable backup. Approved Food Item changes affect future entries and recipes. A diary entry will retain a **small snapshot of its recorded nutritional values**; historic entries need not be recalculated for minor library corrections unless edited. Daily totals may be cached for efficiency.

## 5. Dishes and Meals

Three searchable, independently favouriteable levels: **Food Item → Dish → Meal**. Any level may be recorded directly. Dishes can contain Food Items or other Dishes; Meals can contain Food Items, Dishes, or other Meals. Prevent circular references.

The **Make a Dish** and **Put together a Meal** forms search already-approved components, add them with editable quantities and applicable units (g, mL, defined familiar measures, or a whole nested Dish/Meal), and save only deliberately named recipes into the approved library. Each saved recipe has a calculated total size and nutrition per 100 g (and for the whole recipe), derived from current ingredient nutrition. Treat the mass of measured liquids as approximate where density is unknown. Stored component IDs preserve relationships so later food and recipe edits update linked derived totals. Prevent deletion of ingredients still referenced by recipes. **Capital Case is required for all saved names, including imported foods, dishes, and meals.**

A saved Dish/Meal has defined ingredient quantities. Users can add, omit, scale, or explicitly substitute components; do **not** impose automatic substitution rules. Calculate total Dish weight from ingredient quantities. From an existing Dish or Meal, **Save as variation** opens a new recipe with the existing components but **a blank name**. The user must enter a name that is unique throughout the existing library; do not automatically append “Variation” or any other generated name. It is **linked to its parent by default**, inheriting parent components and future edits except for changed quantities, removed ingredients, or explicit additions; users can deliberately detach it into a standalone recipe. The link checkbox reads **Update details when [parent name] is updated**, accurately naming the parent recipe. Nested variations are supported. Dependency links are based on stable IDs and must reject circular references. Editing one recorded instance does not alter a shared template. New diary entries default to the saved recipe quantities, not the last recorded adjustment.

## 6. Calorie budget and trends

Initially calculate a **maintenance-calorie range** for current body weight, using an evidence-based resting-expenditure estimate plus tracked movement and exercise. Refine estimates cautiously over time from recorded intake and smoothed longer-term weight changes. Do not overfit normal weight fluctuations or presume an incomplete food diary is complete. Show uncertainty where it matters. The budget is calculated, **not manually overridden**.

Weight-loss targets, rates, target dates, and transitions can be developed later; goals in the CSV are **fictional demonstrations**, not adopted personal targets. The CSV now also contains **114 supplied actual weight measurements from 13 February–16 November 2026** in its `weight` column. Only supplied dated measurements have values; missing weigh-ins in this interval are blank and must not be inferred. Other columns remain as demo data. The HTML-included offline sample mirrors those weights. Since this CSV is publicly deployed, do not mistake these measurements for private Firestore records.

## 7. Accounts, storage, and delivery

Use **Google sign-in and Firebase** with separate private personal records for the two users and a shared approved Food Item/Dish/Meal library. Firestore access is limited to **two manually provisioned member UIDs** (no self-enrolment); the Firestore rules make all personal subcollections accessible only to their owner. Shared approved-food changes use real-time listeners and persistent offline caching. Use Google popup login while hosted on GitHub Pages, with a clear signed-in/connected/cached/unauthorised state. Unconfigured or signed-out prototype editing stays local; local records must not be conflated with a signed-in user's records. Entries must work offline and sync when connected, without losing unsynced records. Provide export and **automatic recoverable off-device historical backups**, separate from normal live synchronisation, with almost no ongoing administration. Firestore native managed backups require billing: **no automatic-backup approach is yet agreed or implemented**. Validate a safe no-cost alternative before production use.

Keep the GitHub Pages build lightweight, with vanilla HTML/CSS/JavaScript and a manifest. After the first complete deployment, distribute updates as **patch ZIPs containing changed files only**; unchanged files remain in the existing GitHub Pages repository. Later, package as a signed Android app where necessary for automatic steps; test Health Connect on the intended Xiaomi phones **early**, not after the rest of development. Preserve device and account security and minimise permissions.

**Settings organisation:** The Settings screen contains only three links, in order: **Food library**, **Personal targets**, and **Account**. Account holds Google sign-in/out, connection status, identity, and the manual food-library copy/export, plus reference-data attribution. The Settings screen has no demo-graph CSV upload, reset, or other sample-data controls. Developers manage `dummy-data.csv` outside the app. Any previously stored local CSV override is ignored. All real account records and shared foods are unaffected.

**Personal goal graph lines:** In signed-in mode, any displayed CSV history must use the current account's optional Weight, Water, Steps, Protein, and Fibre goals, **never fictional CSV target values**. This prevents sudden target-line jumps at the last sample day/first real day. The underlying sample intake values are unchanged.

## 8. Prototype status and next work

**Implemented through v0.1.044:** The project owner has configured a separate Firebase project, enabled Google sign-in, published member-only security rules, and authorised both users. Shared approved Food Item synchronisation was verified by the user across phone and computer; the previous temporary food snapshot is no longer seeded. The existing responsive graph and Food Item/Dish/Meal library, NZ/AU/USDA search, barcode/OCR, and recipe composition remain. When signed into an approved account, Weight and Water entry controls now queue private timestamped Firestore documents; the daily chart and current readouts are derived only from that user's events. Weigh-ins are not interpolated; multiple drinks on a day are summed. Settings → Personal targets edits the five optional account-private targets in one form. Signed-out demo charts remain separate. Firestore uses persistent browser caching where supported. In v0.1.041, valid Target saves dismiss the form, all modal content adapts to the mobile keyboard with a single scrollable area, nutritional values in review/recipes are displayed and saved to at most two decimal places, the weight ruler settles on 0.05 kg increments without altering tick marks, and searched Food Items offer a prefilling manual-add row after direct hits.

**Unfinished / not yet production-ready:** Verify the new personal entry and target sync on the owner's live Firebase project, including offline recovery and day-boundary behaviour. Add historical weight/water correction and deletion, a real chronological diary and its normal quick-entry recording flow, exercise logging, Health Connect steps, evidence-based calculated calorie budgets, and reliable no-cost **automatic off-device recoverable historical backups**. Firestore synchronisation and its local cache are not backups. OCR still needs real-label testing. Do not treat personal records as independently backed up until recovery is tested.

**v0.1.042 additions (superseded in part by v0.1.043):** Food Item **Water** first fed the existing water shortcut and the water contribution of recorded (possibly nested) Dishes and Meals; v0.1.043 extends this to explicitly configured Food Items. **v0.1.043–0.1.044 changes:** The Food Item editor offers an optional water-contribution percentage below Scan nutrition panel, flowing through recipes and recorded servings. The Weight band shows an in-range goal line without marker outlines; day tooltips are removed. v0.1.044 removes the scrollbar gutter and visible thumb while retaining scrolling, moves account features into their own page, reorders manual food creation ahead of all external results, requires explicitly unique variation names, clarifies the link checkbox, and makes historical goals consistent with current account targets. No database security rules or existing records are migrated.

**Next focus:** Test Weight, Water, food recording, and Targets between devices and across accounts. Then add historical record editing and the full chronological food diary without disturbing the established visual design. Keep backups a separate, explicit milestone.
